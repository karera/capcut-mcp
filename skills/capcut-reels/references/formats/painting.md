# Format: painting process

Showing the painting process, that painting can be fun, and promoting classes.
Sometimes with a voiceover recorded separately.

## What makes it work
The transformation. Viewers stay to see the blank canvas become a finished piece.
The voice or text adds personality and teaches one thing.

## Structure
1. Hook: show the finished painting (or a striking moment) first, then "here's
   how" back to the blank canvas. Alternatively, open on a satisfying close-up stroke.
2. Process: timelapse of the main stages, with a few real-time moments of
   satisfying strokes, colour mixing, or a key technique.
3. Reveal: the finished piece, held, ideally with a slow push-in or pull-out.
4. Call to action: classes, a question, or "try this". Keep it short.

## Editing choices
- Timelapse: speed up long sections strongly, and slow back to real time for the
  satisfying or instructive moments. Contrast between fast and real time is what
  makes it engaging. Verify sped-up clip timing in CapCut (known tool limit).
- Close-ups of brush, paint, and texture are the best b-roll. Mix wide shots of
  the canvas with close-ups.
- Voiceover: on its own track, cut the picture to match what's being said. Remove
  pauses and false starts in the voice. Keep camera audio low or muted.
- Without a voice: gentle music and short text beats naming each stage, or one
  tip per video.
- Text: stage names or one teaching tip, kept away from the canvas area.
- A warm, consistent filter that flatters colours without changing them much;
  painters' viewers care about true colours.

## For promoting classes
Show people having fun, before and after results from students (with permission),
and a clear, friendly call to action at the end.
