# Format: mood text over music

Example: filmed in a car or a quiet moment, no voice, music playing, a single
statement like "maturing is realising..." on screen.

## What makes it work
The footage sets a feeling, the text delivers a thought the viewer recognises,
and the music carries the emotion. It's simple, so every detail shows.

## Structure
1. Open on the most atmospheric part of the footage with the music already playing.
2. Bring the text in early, within the first second or so. The text is the hook.
3. For a two-part statement, reveal the second half on a musical beat or change.
4. Hold the final line long enough to read twice.
5. Aim for a loop: the end frame and music flow back into the start.

## Editing choices
- Few cuts. One to three shots is often enough. Slow, steady footage suits the mood.
- A gentle slow push-in (scale creeping up slightly) adds life to a static shot.
  If the tools can't animate it, note it as a keyframe for the user.
- One consistent text style, lowercase or sentence case, centred or upper third,
  with a soft fade entrance from the style library.
- A subtle filter for a consistent look across the series.
- No sound effects. Let the music do the work.

## Series thinking
These perform as a series. Keep the same font, position, and filter every time so
viewers recognise the account at a glance.
